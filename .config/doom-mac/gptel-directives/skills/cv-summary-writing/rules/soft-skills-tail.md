This rule defines how to add soft skills (leadership, ownership, adaptability) to a CV summary without breaking the recipe's structural rules. Soft skills are ATS-scored tokens — recruiters and scanners alike weight `ownership`, `mentorship`, and `adaptability` highly for senior IC roles. But they must be added in the right shape: as a tail prepositional modifier, never as a new sentence or a verb-led clause.

## The Tail Modifier Pattern

```
[...main summary...] + "," + "with" + [skill1] + "," + [skill2] + "," + "and" + [skill3 + dimension list] + "."
```

The tail starts with a comma and the preposition `with`. It contains 2-3 concrete soft-skill nouns connected by commas and a final `and`. The last skill often carries a dimension list (`across X, Y, and Z`) that adds breadth.

### Worked Example (matches canonical v12)

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Preposition opener | `with` |
| Skill 1 | `end-to-end ownership` |
| Skill 2 | `mentorship` |
| Skill 3 + dimension list | `fast adaptability across stacks, products, and industries` |

Full tail: `with end-to-end ownership, mentorship, and fast adaptability across stacks, products, and industries.`

### Spanish Equivalent

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Preposition opener | `con` |
| Skill 1 | `ownership end-to-end` |
| Skill 2 | `mentoría` |
| Skill 3 + dimension list | `rápida adaptabilidad entre stacks, productos e industrias` |

Full tail: `con ownership end-to-end, mentoría y rápida adaptabilidad entre stacks, productos e industrias.`

## Approved Soft-Skill Tokens

These are concrete competency names that ATS systems specifically scan for in senior IC candidates. Each is a noun or noun phrase, no verbs.

| Token (EN) | Token (ES) | What it signals |
|---|---|---|
| `end-to-end ownership` | `ownership end-to-end` | Senior IC competency — owns delivery from design through production |
| `ownership of delivery` | `ownership de la entrega` | Variant of end-to-end ownership |
| `mentorship` | `mentoría` | Leadership signal — onboards/develops other engineers |
| `technical leadership` | `liderazgo técnico` | Alternative to mentorship; emphasizes architectural authority |
| `code review leadership` | `liderazgo en revisiones de código` | Specific leadership variant when relevant to candidate experience |
| `fast adaptability` | `rápida adaptabilidad` | Multi-context flexibility — works across stacks, domains, team sizes |
| `cross-functional collaboration` | `colaboración multi-funcional` | Works with product, design, backend, ops — softer than leadership |
| `stakeholder management` | `gestión de stakeholders` | Use for very senior roles (Staff/Principal) |

## Banned Soft-Skill Tokens

These are junior tells, recruiter clichés, or unfalsifiable claims. They DROP the senior-IC signal and trigger immediate rejection.

| Banned token (EN) | Banned token (ES) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `team player` | `jugador de equipo` | Junior tell — every candidate claims this |
| `self-starter` | `autosuficiente` | Junior tell — implies the candidate needs autonomy reassurance |
| `passionate about` | `apasionado por` | Generic enthusiasm claim — unfalsifiable |
| `strong communicator` | `buen comunicador` | Generic — show via writing samples, don't claim |
| `proven leader` | `líder probado` | Cliché — same family as "Proven track record" |
| `results-oriented` | `orientado a resultados` | Marketing cliché |
| `detail-oriented` | `detallista` | Generic |
| `quick learner` | `aprendizaje rápido` | Junior tell — implies the candidate is still learning |
| `hard worker` | `trabajador` | Junior tell |

## The Adaptability Dimension List

The third soft skill (when adaptability is included) takes a dimension list that emphasizes the candidate's breadth.

### Canonical dimension triad

`across stacks, products, and industries` / `entre stacks, productos e industrias`

This covers:
- **stacks** — Tech stacks (FE/BE/FS, multiple frameworks, language polyglot)
- **products** — Product types (e-commerce, SaaS, marketing, internal tools, microservices)
- **industries** — Business sectors (B2C, B2B, agency, startup, enterprise)

### When to vary the dimension list

| Candidate context | Suggested dimensions |
|---|---|
| Multi-framework engineer with broad domain experience | `stacks, products, and industries` (canonical) |
| Backend specialist who's worked across many teams | `services, domains, and team sizes` |
| Frontend specialist who's done multiple product types | `frameworks, design systems, and product domains` |
| Full-stack consultant with many clients | `stacks, clients, and engagement models` |
| Engineer at one company across many roles | `teams, product areas, and engineering disciplines` |

**Rule:** Always 3 dimensions. Two dimensions reads incomplete; four+ dimensions reads as keyword-stuffing.

## Pattern Variations

### Two soft skills only (when the candidate doesn't have mentorship/leadership experience)

```
..., with end-to-end ownership and fast adaptability across stacks, products, and industries.
```

### Three soft skills with leadership variant

```
..., with end-to-end ownership, technical leadership, and fast adaptability across stacks, products, and industries.
```

### Three soft skills emphasizing cross-functional work

```
..., with end-to-end ownership, cross-functional collaboration, and fast adaptability across stacks, products, and industries.
```

## Correct vs. Incorrect Examples

### Example A — Wrong: Soft Skills as New Sentence

**Incorrect:**
> ...AI-assisted development in fully remote Agile teams. **Strong sense of ownership and adaptability.**

This violates the one-sentence mandate. The soft skills are tacked on as a fragment.

**Correct:**
> ...AI-assisted development in fully remote Agile teams**, with end-to-end ownership, mentorship, and fast adaptability across stacks, products, and industries.**

### Example B — Wrong: Verb-Led Soft Skills

**Incorrect:**
> ...AI-assisted development in fully remote Agile teams. **Leads** through ownership and **drives** adaptability across teams.

Violates Rule 2 (no standalone verbs). `Leads` and `drives` imply unstated subjects.

**Correct:** Use the tail modifier instead.
> ...AI-assisted development in fully remote Agile teams, with end-to-end ownership, **technical leadership**, and fast adaptability across stacks, products, and industries.

### Example C — Wrong: Soft Skills Before the Tech Stack

**Incorrect:**
> Senior Engineer **with strong ownership and adaptability** with 8+ years building platforms using *Vue 3*...

Soft skills placed at the front break the recipe order. The [Identity & Experience] slot must come first, soft skills come at the END.

**Correct:** Move soft skills to the tail.
> *Senior Engineer* with 8+ years building platforms using *Vue 3*... in fully remote Agile teams, **with end-to-end ownership and fast adaptability across stacks, products, and industries**.

### Example D — Wrong: Banned Junior Tells

**Incorrect:**
> ...in fully remote Agile teams, with **passion**, **a strong work ethic**, and **adaptability**.

`passion`, `strong work ethic`, `quick learner`, etc. are banned junior tells.

**Correct:** Use concrete senior-IC tokens.
> ...in fully remote Agile teams, with **end-to-end ownership**, **mentorship**, and **fast adaptability across stacks, products, and industries**.

## ATS Scoring Notes

Modern ATS systems (Greenhouse AI, Workday AI, Lever AI) weight soft-skill tokens differently for senior tiers:

| Tier | Soft-skill tokens that tier up the score |
|---|---|
| Junior (0-3 yrs) | `quick learner`, `team player` actually score positively here (they signal entry-level fit) |
| Mid (4-7 yrs) | `cross-functional collaboration`, `ownership of delivery` |
| Senior (7+ yrs) | `end-to-end ownership`, `technical leadership`, `mentorship`, `adaptability` |
| Staff/Principal (10+ yrs) | All senior tokens PLUS `stakeholder management`, `architecture leadership`, `org-wide impact` |

The canonical worked example targets the Senior tier — the three tokens (ownership, mentorship, adaptability) are precisely the senior-tier signals.
